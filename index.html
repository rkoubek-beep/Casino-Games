Welcome to Craps

Craps is a fast-paced dice game played on a large felt table. Players wager on the outcome of two dice. The game has two phases: the Come-Out Roll and the Point Phase. With the right bets, the house edge can be as low as 1.36%.

— Craps Reference & Strategy —

Pass Line — The Foundation Bet ▼
The cornerstone of craps. House edge: 1.41%. Come-out roll: 7 or 11 wins instantly; 2, 3, 12 lose. Any other number sets the Point — roll it again before a 7 to win. Strategy: Always start here. Never skip it. Combined with Free Odds (available in real casinos), the effective edge drops below 1%.
Don't Pass — The Dark Side ▼
Slightly better odds at 1.36% edge. You win when others lose (7 after a Point). Pushes on 12 (bar). Socially unpopular at live tables since you're rooting against the shooter, but mathematically superior to Pass. Strategy: Excellent choice for math-focused players who don't mind going solo.

Field, Hardways & Props — The Trap Bets ▼
Field (5.6% edge), Any Craps (11.1%), Any 7 (16.9%), Hardways (9–11%). These bets are flashy and frequently advertised by casinos for a reason — they're profitable for the house. Strategy: Treat proposition bets as entertainment only, never as a core strategy. A $5 Any 7 now and then is fine; betting it every roll will drain your stack fast.
🏆 Optimal Craps Strategy ▼
Tier 1 (Best): Pass Line or Don't Pass only. Simple, low edge, longest playing time per dollar.
Tier 2 (Good): Pass Line + Place 6 & 8. More action with still-reasonable edges.
Tier 3 (Okay): Adding Come bets for multiple numbers working at once.
Avoid: Any 7, Any Craps, Big 6/8 (inferior to Place 6/8), Hardways as your primary bet.
Rule of thumb: If the house edge is over 5%, that bet is costing you real money.

— Blackjack Reference & Strategy —

Card Values & Aces ▼
Number cards = face value. J, Q, K = 10 each. Ace = 1 or 11 (whichever helps more). A "soft" hand contains an Ace counted as 11 (Soft 17 = A+6). A "hard" hand has no Ace, or the Ace counts as 1. Blackjack = Ace + 10-value card on the deal = pays 3:2. Strategy: Treat aces as 11 until you'd bust — then count them as 1.
Dealer Rules You Must Know ▼
The dealer plays by fixed rules: must hit on 16 or less, must stand on hard 17+. Most tables require dealer to hit Soft 17 (A+6). This rule adds ~0.2% to the house edge. The dealer only reveals one card (the "up-card") — your strategy depends heavily on what that card is. Key insight: Dealer busts most often when showing 4, 5, or 6. These are your best opportunities to double down.
Your Four Moves Explained ▼
Hit: Take another card. Use when your total is low or the dealer shows a strong card (7–Ace).
Stand: Keep your hand. Use on strong totals or when the dealer shows 4–6 (likely to bust).
Double Down: Double your bet and take exactly one more card. Best on hard 11, hard 10, and hard 9 against weak dealer cards.
Split: When dealt a pair, separate into two hands with equal bets. Always split Aces and 8s. Never split 10s or 5s.
Insurance — Almost Always a Trap ▼
When the dealer shows an Ace, you can bet up to half your original bet that the dealer has Blackjack (pays 2:1). The math: Only 4 of 13 card denominations are 10-value, so the probability of dealer BJ is ~30.8%. Insurance pays 2:1 but breaks even only at 33.3%. Result: insurance carries a 7.7% house edge. Strategy: Never take insurance unless you're a card counter with a very high count. Even "even money" (insurance on your own BJ) costs you money long-term.
